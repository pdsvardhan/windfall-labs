# Can Value + Momentum run on a free P/E? (iteration 201, 2026-10-20)

Measured on master 7b47b40 (after the adr-050 switch and the 20-Oct Trendlyne refresh). Measurement
only — nothing in the engine or the paper books changed. Scripts: `backend/scripts/free_pe/`;
results: `data/free_pe.duckdb` (`pe_agreement`, `pe_backtests`). Not investment advice.

**The two P/Es.** `tl_pe` is Trendlyne's daily trailing-twelve-month P/E — current, but survivors
only and harvested from the browser. `pe` is the engine's own: adjusted close / latest *annual* EPS
from the screener.in store, known 120 days after the fiscal year ends — free, includes delisted
companies, excludes banks/NBFCs, but up to 16 months stale by construction.

## 1. Do they agree? (item 1464)

Monthly, Jul 2016 – Aug 2026, on the point-in-time ₹500 cr universe:

| | 2016* | 2018 | 2020 | 2022 | 2024 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| universe | 836 | 933 | 816 | 1,171 | 1,469 | 1,655 |
| free `pe` covers | 432 | 488 | 460 | 595 | 735 | 822 |
| `tl_pe` covers | 646 | 754 | 663 | 990 | 1,246 | 1,510 |
| rank correlation (both) | 0.73 | 0.82 | 0.85 | 0.85 | 0.82 | 0.82 |
| cheapest fifth in common | 65% | 78% | 78% | 76% | 78% | 77% |

\* 2016 = Oct–Dec. In Jul–Sep 2016 Trendlyne had P/Es for only 7–12 names; those months are
excluded here and from the averages.

Where both exist they rank stocks much the same: correlation 0.82, and three-quarters of the
cheapest fifth in common (so a quarter differs). The difference is **coverage**: the free P/E sees
about half the universe. In 2026, 760 universe names are not in the screener.in store at all
(it holds 1,474 companies; 4 more have only quarantined rows), and 176 of the uncovered names are
banks/NBFCs, which it excludes by design. Trendlyne's extra
coverage includes 27 → 166 banks/NBFCs over the decade. The free P/E does see 13–45 delisted names
a year (fewer recently) that Trendlyne has dropped.

## 2. Does the strategy hold up? (item 1465)

`CMP_valmom_m_20` (P/E ascending + 6-month momentum, top 20, monthly, same costs). Each run done
twice; identical calls differ by up to ~0.6 points (to-do #967). CAGR / Sharpe / max drawdown:

| window | live book as configured | Trendlyne P/E required | free P/E required | momentum only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 years 2016–26 | 25.3% / 0.99 / −67.6% | 18.4% / 0.77 / −68.2% | **23.5% / 0.97 / −66.2%** | 28.6% / 1.07 / −52.6% |
| post-COVID 5y | 17.1% / 0.74 / −33.3% | 19.5% / 0.80 / −37.4% | **22.2% / 0.93 / −35.2%** | 18.3% / 0.77 / −37.3% |
| pre-COVID 5y | 28.9% / 1.10 / −67.0% | 16.4% / 0.70 / −69.2% | **22.4% / 0.92 / −67.3%** | 37.2% / 1.31 / −52.8% |

**Correction.** An earlier version of this table compared the two P/Es without requiring a P/E.
The blend quietly ranks a stock that has no P/E on momentum alone, so 93% of that "free P/E" book's
buys had no free P/E — it was a momentum test. The independent verifier caught it; the table above
requires a positive P/E in both columns.

What it shows:

- **On a fair test the free P/E did better than Trendlyne's in every window** — by 5.1, 2.7 and 6.0
  points a year, with better Sharpe. Part of that may be *which* stocks it can see (no banks/NBFCs,
  half the universe) rather than the quality of the number; the two books share only 14–24% of
  their holdings. Both sides carry an unmeasured bias: Trendlyne's P/E exists only for companies
  that survived, and the free P/E uses screener.in's figures as restated *today* (e.g. HARDWYN's
  EPS rebased ×0.70–0.75 across all years), which leaks later information into the past.
- **The live book is a different strategy from its name.** Without a P/E requirement it fills
  slots with names that have no Trendlyne P/E, on momentum alone. Over ten years that was 367
  entries: about half loss-makers, four in ten companies that later delisted (which Trendlyne
  never carried). Since COVID about two-thirds were loss-makers (ATHERENERG, BLUSPRING, EXICOM
  entered in Jul–Oct 2026). Over ten years it helped (25.3% vs 18.4%), and the gap comes from 2020
  and 2021 (+100% vs +22%, +147% vs +93%), not the early years. Over the post-COVID window as a
  whole it hurt: 17.1% vs 19.5%.
- In Jul–Sep 2016 the Trendlyne-P/E books in the 10-year and pre-COVID runs are effectively
  momentum-only, because Trendlyne had almost no P/Es yet.
- Momentum alone beat every value variant over ten years and pre-COVID, but not post-COVID.

## 3. A free trailing-twelve-month P/E? (item 1466)

- **NSE financial-results filings** (legacy endpoint): quarterly back to 2005; machine-readable
  XBRL from 2017–2018 (26–28 quarters per company) to Dec 2024.
- **NSE integrated filings** (2025 onwards): iXBRL to the latest quarter (TCS: 8 Oct 2026), with a
  broadcast timestamp — so point in time.
- Both carry quarterly profit, basic EPS and revenue as tags (checked end to end on TCS only;
  small/SME companies and banks, which file under a different taxonomy, are untested).
- **screener.in's free page** shows only 13 quarters.

So a free, point-in-time TTM P/E is feasible from about 2018 to today — enough for the post-COVID
window, not for 2016–17. Not built this round: it is ~1,900 companies × ~32 filings ≈ 60,000
polite fetches (~20 hours) plus an XBRL parser. Proposed as its own task.

## 4. Can the screener.in store refresh on a schedule? (item 1467)

Yes: plain HTTP, 40 companies in 106 s (2.6 s each), no failures — the whole store in about an hour
(rate limits over 1,474 names untested). A run now adds nothing new (FY2026 was fetched in June).
What changes on a refresh is mostly per-share rebasing (EPS restated for later splits, mostly in
pre-listing years); real restatements were 5 rows in 40 companies, and the confidence flips came
from the third-party yfinance cross-check. Because a refresh overwrites, a scheduled one should keep
a dated copy of each run. Proposed (not installed): monthly, 03:00 on the 5th, May–September.

## 5. Recommendation (item 1468)

1. **No switch yet; the free P/E was not weaker in these tests.** On a fair test it beat Trendlyne's,
   but the universe it sees and the restatement bias could explain some of that. Before switching, remove the universe difference: extend the screener.in store to the rest of
   the universe (~760 companies, ~590 of them not banks; about 35 minutes), exclude banks/NBFCs on
   both sides, and re-run this comparison on equal terms.
2. **Decide what Value + Momentum should be.** As configured it buys loss-makers on momentum alone.
   Requiring a P/E made it better since COVID and worse over ten years. That is a strategy choice
   for you, separate from the data source.
3. **Optional:** build the free TTM P/E (2018 →) from NSE filings (~20 hours of fetching), and
   schedule the screener.in refresh with dated snapshots.

The browser harvest stays either way — the D/V/M scores have no free equivalent.
